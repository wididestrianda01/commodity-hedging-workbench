# Interview Brief — pre-answered questions

Extension of the Citi MQA Forage simulation onto frozen real data (Projects-section line, no Citi affiliation claimed). Numbers below are the report's verified numbers (`report/report_numbers.json`, regenerated by `report/make_numbers.py`; hand-checks in `tests/`).

## Convenience yield vs storage cost

The cost-of-carry identity is $F_2 = F_1 \exp((r + d - y)\tau)$. Spreads alone give total carry; separating $y$ from $d$ needs a storage-outlay assumption, so the workbench reports yield gross of storage ($d=0$, stated). On the frozen coffee curve the implied yield is 40.8%/yr at the front decaying to 6.3%/yr on the back — the market pays a large convenience yield for near coffee (inventory scarcity signal), and the curve is backwardated at every consecutive spread as of 2026-09-04.

## EE vs PFE

EE(t) is the expected positive exposure at time t — the risk-neutral average of max(MtM, 0) across paths; PFE(t) is a tail quantile (95th percentile) of that same distribution. On the 2.67-contract book: EPE $33,755 vs 1-year PFE $250,975. CVA integrates EE over time; collateral thresholds are set against PFE. EE ≤ PFE holds by construction and is asserted in the suite.

## Why a collar (not just futures)

The full futures hedge locks the purchase cost exactly — $3,891,000 on 1.2M lb at 324.25 ¢/lb — but carries the full margin-funding risk: 95th-percentile peak $103,797. The zero-cost collar (long 280 put / short 382.06 call, both at 7.69 ¢/lb, premium gap ~1e-14) locks the same cost between the strikes while capping margin funding at the put protection — 95th-percentile peak $44,250, ~58% lower. The trade-off is explicit: beyond the strikes the cost moves 1:1 with the market again (p95 cost $4.595M vs the futures' locked $3.891M). Pick futures for budget certainty, collar for a margin-constrained buyer. Key subtlety worth stating in an interview: the strikes bound the hedge P&L, not the buyer's cost.

## Netting

Under a netting agreement the exposure of a set of positions is the net MtM, not the sum of gross exposures: netted CVA ≤ sum of standalone CVAs (asserted in the suite). For the roaster program the relevant netting set is the futures positions across contract months; for the desk framing it's why ISDA netting collapses multibillion gross exposures into a fraction. Collateral then attenuates the netted profile monotonically (threshold + independent amount).

## Wrong-way risk

WWR is correlation between counterparty default and exposure. In this book it is benign by inspection: the roaster buys physical and hedges long — its exposure to us grows when coffee rises, i.e. when the roaster's own business (a buyer of coffee) is stressed, which is right-way risk for us as issuer of the note's short call. The dangerous direction would be selling the participation note's upside to a producer long coffee — exposure and counterparty distress would then correlate. Kept qualitative per scope; modelling it needs a correlated default-price process.
